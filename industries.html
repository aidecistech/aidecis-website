Choose your industry

Different industries. The same need for accountable decisions.

Automotive

Automotive supply chains connect many supplier tiers with just-in-time and just-in-sequence production. EVs add battery materials, source requirements and battery regulation. One late part or unapproved substitution can affect an entire production schedule.

↗

Pharma and biotech

GMP requirements, temperature-sensitive shipments and serialisation create a chain where evidence matters at every hand-off. International dependence on active ingredients adds exposure. A deviation may require a batch hold, a quality review or a recall.

↗

Food and beverage

Perishable and seasonal materials meet volatile commodity prices, food-safety requirements and retailer commitments. A delivery change can affect shelf life, production, waste and service at the same time. Teams need to see the whole trade-off before committing.

↗

Contract logistics

3PL and e-commerce fulfilment operators coordinate multiple client networks, sites and service agreements. Rerouting, returns and customs exceptions move quickly. Each decision needs the right client rules, spending authority and accountable owner.

↗

Aerospace and defense

Long lead times, specialised suppliers and part traceability make aerospace and defense supply chains unforgiving. Production ramp-up and maintenance add pressure to scarce components. Changes must respect certification requirements, approved sources and applicable export restrictions.

↗

Semiconductors and electronics

Semiconductor and electronics production spans many process steps, countries and specialised suppliers. Concentrated manufacturing and equipment capacity can turn a local disruption into a network-wide shortage. Allocation and alternative sourcing need an explicit trade-off.

↗

Medical devices

Medical-device supply chains combine specialised suppliers, identification and traceability requirements, and validated production or sterilisation steps. A component change can affect safety, documentation and supply continuity. Approval needs to stay connected to the evidence.

↗

Chemicals and specialty chemicals

Chemical supply chains connect feedstock availability, continuous processes and bulk or tank logistics. Hazardous materials and compatibility constraints make a quick workaround consequential. Teams need cost, safety evidence and operating authority in the same decision.

↗

Machinery and industrial equipment

High-mix, low-volume and engineer-to-order production brings many unique parts, engineering changes and project commitments. Long service lives add spare-parts dependencies. Local choices need to remain connected to cost, specification and delivery consequences.

↗

Fashion, textiles and retail

Short collection cycles, large product ranges and fragmented supplier networks make timing and visibility difficult. A late shipment can force markdowns or costly air freight. Sourcing transparency and due-diligence obligations add evidence requirements that vary by market and company.
