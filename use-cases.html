Logistics · reference dossier

Expedite freight after disruption

Trigger / contextLate estimated arrival, line-stoppage risk, order value at risk, and carrier quotes and capacity.

Aidecis actionCompare expedited-shipping options against total disruption cost and delegated approval limits. Send the decision to logistics and finance for approval. Issue a time- and scope-limited TMS booking authorization, then reconcile the invoice against the approved booking and record downtime avoided.

Value evidenceDecision time; avoided downtime; premium spend; invoice variance; SLA compliance.

Inventory · allocation

Inventory reallocation

Trigger / contextShortage at one site, surplus at another, demand, shelf life, transit and transfer cost.

Aidecis actionRank receiving sites by service risk. Protect minimum stock and reserved orders, then prioritize transfers by value and impact. Create a WMS or ERP transfer only after approval.

Value evidenceFill rate; stockout hours; transfer cost; excess inventory; policy exceptions.

Planning · allocation

Demand surge allocation

Trigger / contextA demand signal or large order exceeds the supply available to promise.

Aidecis actionCompare allocation options against customer commitments, margin, strategic rules and fairness. Record who approved any change to a customer promise, then update planning or ERP after approval.

Value evidenceOn-time-in-full; revenue protected; promise changes; allocation overrides.

Procurement · resilience

Supplier disruption and alternate source

Trigger / contextSupplier delay, capacity cut, credit alert or geopolitical disruption.

Aidecis actionMap affected materials and plants. Compare approved alternatives, lead times and qualification status. Require quality and procurement approval, then limit any purchase-order change to the approved scope.

Value evidenceDays of cover; production protected; premium purchase cost; alternate qualification time.

Quality · disposition

Quality hold, release or substitute

Trigger / contextQMS hold, lab result, nonconformance or product shortage.

Aidecis actionLink affected lots and orders to downstream customers. Block unsafe releases, route the disposition decision to authorized quality and operations owners, and record the evidence for any substitution or release.

Value evidenceTime to disposition; affected volume; compliance deviation; scrap and service impact.

Manufacturing · recovery

Production rescheduling

Trigger / contextMachine downtime, labor absence, material constraint, constrained line or urgent order.

Aidecis actionCompare feasible schedules and their downstream effects. Respect labor, maintenance, quality and customer constraints. Require approval from the production owner, then write approved schedule changes to MES or APS.

Value evidenceSchedule adherence; changeover loss; recovery time; overtime; customer impact.

Logistics · routing

Logistics reroute or carrier choice

Trigger / contextLane disruption, port congestion, weather, carrier capacity or service failure.

Aidecis actionCompare route and carrier options by service, cost, emissions and contractual limits. Check approved-carrier and site caps. After approval, send the exact booking change to TMS.

Value evidenceTransit time; premium cost; service reliability; reroute delay; carrier compliance.

Customer service · prioritization

Customer promise and backorder priority

Trigger / contextDemand exceeds supply, or a high-priority order risks missing its required delivery date.

Aidecis actionShow affected customers and order dependencies. Apply the client priority policy and propose revised delivery promises, split shipments or substitutions. Require approval from an authorized commercial or operations owner.

Value evidenceOTIF; promise accuracy; revenue protected; fairness and override rate.

Procurement · spend control

Procurement price, quantity or PO exception

Trigger / contextA supplier requests a price change, excess quantity or expedited purchase outside agreed tolerances.

Aidecis actionCompare contract terms, budget, inventory, supply criticality and alternatives. Route approval according to the total commitment and delegated authority. Amend the purchase order only within the approved scope.

Value evidencePrice variance; maverick spend; approval time; supply continuity.

Finance · reconciliation

Invoice variance, claim and close-out

Trigger / contextAn invoice differs from the quote, purchase order, receipt or approved expedited shipment.

Aidecis actionMatch the approved dossier authorization and actual execution against the quote, purchase order and receipt. Send out-of-tolerance differences to finance, prepare evidence for a supplier claim or chargeback, and close the case only after reconciliation.

Value evidenceLeakage recovered; claim success; days to close; false disputes; unapproved spend.

Maintenance · asset failure

Unplanned failure: run reduced or stop to repair

Trigger / contextA priority-1 maintenance notification on a critical asset, such as a seal leak on a refinery feed pump, with the spare part days away.

Aidecis actionCompare running at a reduced rate, stopping to repair and switching to standby equipment on lost throughput, margin, safety, permits and spare-part lead time. Route the choice to the site owner within the stop-duration envelope, then schedule the work order and the rate plan only after approval.

Value evidenceThroughput protected; margin impact; time to decision; safety deviations; repeat failures.

Trading · decision chain

Buy a cargo, and the decisions it sets in motion

Trigger / contextA trader sees a spread to buy an extra cargo against a monthly position limit; the purchase will need tank space, transport and an accrual downstream.

Aidecis actionCheck the commitment against the position limit and route it to the head of trading. Once approved, open the downstream decisions for tank allocation and barge nomination, each with its own owner, and keep the whole chain visible in one register.

Value evidencePosition within limits; downstream decisions on time; demurrage avoided; margin realised.

The decision library

Seventy-one decisions in twelve families.

A decision belongs here when it crosses someone's authority, is hard to undo, affects others downstream, commits the company outside or puts safety, quality or compliance at stake. Each one has a trigger, a limit that decides who approves, typed options, a scoped action and an outcome to reconcile.

Trading and commodity positions

  • Buy or sell a cargo or parcel Flagship
  • Hedge or close a position
  • Breach of a position or credit limit
  • Term contract nomination or volume flex
  • Accept or reject an off-spec cargo
  • Counterparty exception (new, credit, sanctions)

Procurement and supply

  • Ingredient or material price beyond envelope Flagship
  • Alternate source after supplier disruption
  • Spot buy outside contract
  • PO price, quantity or date exception
  • Fast-track supplier qualification
  • Supplier force majeure response
  • Contract award or extension

Demand and customer commitments

  • Demand surge allocation
  • Customer promise and backorder priority Flagship
  • Accept an order outside policy (rush, MOQ, credit)
  • Product substitution for a customer
  • Price or discount exception
  • Customer claim or penalty settlement

Planning and inventory

  • Inventory reallocation between sites Flagship
  • Safety-stock override
  • Build-ahead or pre-buy
  • Plan change after the S&OP freeze
  • Write-off, rework or scrap
  • Re-source a product to another site

Production and operations

  • Production rescheduling Flagship
  • Rate cut or line stop
  • Extra shift or overtime
  • Recipe, BOM or material substitution
  • Outsource or toll manufacturing
  • Accept a yield or process deviation

Maintenance and assets

  • Unplanned failure: run reduced or stop to repair Flagship
  • Machine breakdown: repair, replace or rent
  • Defer planned maintenance
  • Express spare part
  • Use standby or bypass equipment past inspection
  • Turnaround scope or date change
  • Condition-based intervention

Energy and utilities

  • Curtail or shift production on an energy price spike Flagship
  • Fuel or feedstock switch
  • Respond to a grid or utility outage
  • Buy or sell energy or capacity

Quality and product compliance

  • Quality hold, release or substitute Flagship
  • Concession to a customer specification
  • Recall or market withdrawal
  • Accept a raw material outside specification
  • Regulatory notification

Health, safety and environment

  • Permit or emission limit exceedance (e.g. flaring) Flagship
  • Management of change for a temporary deviation
  • Stop work or evacuate on a safety risk
  • Dangerous goods exception

Logistics and transport

  • Expedite freight after disruption Flagship
  • Reroute or carrier choice
  • Mode shift (road, rail, sea, air)
  • Vessel or barge nomination and laycan
  • Demurrage vs waiting vs diversion
  • Customs or trade compliance exception
  • Cross-dock or direct-ship exception

Warehousing and terminals

  • Tank or storage allocation Flagship
  • Storage overflow or external storage
  • Blend, upgrade or downgrade product in tank
  • Quarantine or stock block
  • Loading slot priority

Finance and close-out

  • Invoice variance, claim and close-out Flagship
  • Supplier or carrier chargeback
  • Budget overrun approval
  • Accrual or provision for a disruption
  • Release a customer credit hold
  • Write-off of a receivable or claim
  • Insurance claim after an incident
  • Emergency spend outside the delegation of authority

The library holds the structure of each decision, not the answer: your own limits, systems and history are added with you, and examples stay labelled illustrative until real cases prove them.
