Decision accountability in supply chains

Why Your Supply-Chain Decisions Lack Accountability (And How Aidecis Fixes It)

Every day, your teams make consequential supply-chain decisions: reroute a shipment, expedite a line, swap a supplier, adjust inventory. Most of them happen in email threads, spreadsheets, and Slack. Most lack clear ownership. Most are invisible until something breaks.

Aidecis replaces fragmented approval loops with one accountable decision record: who had authority, what policy applied, who approved it, what actually executed, and what happened next.

The 10 Challenges

Where accountability breaks down.

Each scenario starts as an everyday operating decision. The risk appears when ownership, policy, evidence or execution gets separated.

01

Approval limit exceeded. Decision proceeds anyway.

A reroute costs 40% more than authority allows. It ships anyway. Finance notices 3 weeks later. Aidecis flags the overage, holds execution, routes to the right approver.

02

Local decision. Global consequences nobody saw.

A supplier swap cuts cost at Plant A. It silently breaks quality rules at Plant B, a customer spec at Plant C, inventory at Plant D. Discovered too late. Aidecis surfaces downstream impacts before execution.

03

Missing or stale information. Decision made anyway.

A reroute gets approved without current carrier capacity. A production change goes live without the latest quality tolerances. Aidecis flags incomplete evidence and pauses for review.

04

Rule broken. Discovered after execution.

A discount violates a customer exclusivity clause. A shipment routing breaks a service commitment. A supplier expedite contradicts policy. Damage done. Aidecis checks every decision against contracts, regulations and safety rules before execution.

05

No clear owner. Decision stalls or lands wrong.

A complicated approval bounces between logistics, finance and operations. No one knows who decides. It either stalls or gets silently decided by whoever had access. Aidecis surfaces when ownership is missing and prompts assignment.

06

Same situation, different decisions across sites.

Site A approves expedites at 15% cost premium. Site B at 8%. Site C doesn't allow them. No one knows why they differ. Aidecis flags unexplained variation, so justified differences stay and drift gets visible.

07

Same exception approved five times this month.

People keep overriding the approval process for the same disruption. Or bypassing compliance because it adds 3 days to a 2-day window. Aidecis identifies recurring exceptions and escalates them to process owners—so workarounds become a signal to fix the system.

08

Disruption hits. Wrong trade-off gets made in panic.

A supplier fails. A port closes. Your team scrambles for the fastest solution and picks one that costs 40% more or breaches a commitment. Aidecis surfaces available options against your service, cost, resilience and compliance priorities during the disruption.

09

This decision works 47 times. Could a bot handle it now?

Over 12 months, the team makes the same decision with 95% consistent outcomes and only rare exceptions. The work is pure pattern recognition. Aidecis learns from evidence and results, recommending whether this decision is stable enough for bounded automation.

10

Approval given. Order never reaches the warehouse.

Finance approves an expedite. The planner is notified. But the request never reaches the carrier because the message format doesn't match the WMS API. Aidecis routes only the approved action through your connected system and records the execution.

The pattern

Decision + Evidence + Approval + Execution + Outcome, in one accountable chain.

Every crisis above can be prevented if one thing is true: the decision is seen before execution, checked against authority and policy, approved by the right person, and routed safely to the system that acts on it.

That's what Aidecis does.
